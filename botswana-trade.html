Market access & border rules

Where Botswana traders can look

SACU customs union

Botswana's customs relationship with South Africa, Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini shapes regional sourcing and border costs. Product-specific rules, VAT and excise still matter.

BURS trade agreements ↗

SADC Free Trade Area

Preferences are conditional. Goods must satisfy rules of origin; traders need the appropriate registration and certificate of origin for consignments claiming SADC preferences.

AfCFTA

The continental market can widen opportunities, but preferences depend on tariff line, partner-country implementation, rules of origin and customs procedures.

Commercial research framework

Opportunity screens for Botswana businesses

Import substitution

Study recurring food, beverage, construction-input, machinery and household-goods imports. Check local capacity, input costs, standards, utilities, logistics and demand.

Value-added minerals

Investigate downstream processing, repair, engineering, testing, packaging and services around diamonds, copper and other mineral value chains. Test capital needs and export scale.

Agri-food exports

Assess livestock, meat, horticulture and processed foods against sanitary requirements, cold-chain costs, buyer specifications, traceability and market access.

Regional distribution

Map demand and transport corridors into South Africa, Namibia, Zambia and Zimbabwe. Model border delays, fuel, insurance, warehousing, payment risk and origin rules.

Before investing: calculate landed cost and gross margin; verify HS code and tariff; confirm permits and standards; obtain buyer evidence; model currency and transport shocks; stress-test working capital. A headline is not a business plan.
